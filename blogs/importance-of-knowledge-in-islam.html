The Importance of Knowledge in Islam

Islam begins with a command to read. The very first word revealed to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was Iqra — "Read". That one word tells us how central knowledge is in our faith: before prayer, before fasting, before any other command, Allah told His Messenger to read.

Knowledge in the Quran and Hadith

Allah says in the Quran: "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (39:9). The Prophet ﷺ said, "Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim." Knowledge here is not only religious learning — it includes every useful knowledge that helps people live well and serve others.

What kind of knowledge?

First, the knowledge of our faith: the Quran, the life of the Prophet ﷺ, prayer, and the rulings we need every day. Second, the knowledge of the world: medicine, science, language, trade and craft. The early Muslim civilisation excelled in both, because it saw no wall between them.

Knowledge with manners

Knowledge without humility becomes pride. The scholars of the past taught that a student should be humble, patient and honest — and that knowledge must lead to good action. Learning a verse and living by it is better than memorising a book and ignoring it.

Start small and stay regular: a page of the Quran with its meaning each day, one hadith, one question asked to a teacher. Over a year, that small habit becomes a large store of knowledge.
