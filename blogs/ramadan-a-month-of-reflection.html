Ramadan — A Month of Reflection, Fasting and Spiritual Renewal

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic calendar, the month in which the Quran was first revealed. For one month, Muslims fast from dawn to sunset — no food, no drink — and turn their attention to prayer, the Quran and charity.

What is fasting?

Fasting is not only about hunger. It is training for the heart: to control the tongue, to lower the gaze, to give to others, and to remember those who have little all year round. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever does not give up false speech and evil actions, Allah has no need of his leaving food and drink.

A daily routine for Ramadan

Begin the day with Suhoor before Fajr, pray on time, read a portion of the Quran every day — one Juz a day completes the Quran in the month — and pray Taraweeh at night. Give charity, even a little, every day, and use the last ten nights to seek Laylat-ul-Qadr, the Night of Power, which is better than a thousand months.

For children and beginners

Children can start with half-day fasts and join the family for Iftar. The goal is love for the month, not hardship. Ramadan is a guest that visits once a year — welcome it with a plan, and let it leave you better than it found you.
