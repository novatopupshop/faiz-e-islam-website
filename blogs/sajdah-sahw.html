Sajdah Sahw — What to Do If You Forget How Many Rak'ahs You Prayed

Forgetting how many rak'ahs you have prayed happens to almost everyone. Islam has a clear remedy for it: Sajdah Sahw, the two prostrations of forgetfulness.

When is Sajdah Sahw needed?

If you add an extra rak'ah, miss a wajib act, or you are unsure whether you prayed three or four rak'ahs, Sajdah Sahw corrects the prayer. If you only doubted for a moment and then became sure, no Sajdah Sahw is needed.

What to do if you are unsure of the number

Scholars teach that when you cannot decide, you should act on the smaller number — the one you are certain about — and complete the prayer, then do Sajdah Sahw. For example, if you are unsure between three and four, count it as three, pray one more rak'ah, then do Sajdah Sahw before the Salam.

How to perform it

After the final Tashahhud, say the Salam to one side (or complete the prayer as your school teaches), then make two extra prostrations with the usual tasbih, sit, recite Tashahhud again, and end with Salam. The exact form differs slightly between schools of thought, so follow the practice of your local scholars.

The lesson is simple: do not abandon the prayer because of a mistake. Allah has given us a way to fix it and carry on.
